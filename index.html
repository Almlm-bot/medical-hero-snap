( A ) — Stillness

A chair that holds the day still

The first room is not a showpiece. It is a pause: plaster, timber, and a single sculptural seat that asks you to sit longer than you planned. Light moves across the wall and nothing else is required.

( B ) — The cabin

A dark house in the pines

Black timber, a low deck, and grass that grows right to the edge. The cabin is designed to disappear into the trees so the forest remains the louder room. Evenings end with glass, quiet, and the smell of resin.

( C ) — The shore

Where the roof meets the tide

A gabled house on stone and sand, set against a single boulder and a long dusk. This is the place the headline is written for: slower days, salt air, and a horizon that does not hurry you.

( D ) — The nook

A window that reads the forest

A deep chair, a tripod lamp, and a curtain that filters the pines. This room is for staying: books, rain, and the kind of quiet that makes memories feel longer than the hours that made them.

( E ) — The terrace

Warm stone, still water

At the edge of the property the day cools into steam and gold. The bath is a threshold between house and landscape — a last room outdoors, where meaning stays because you finally stop moving.
